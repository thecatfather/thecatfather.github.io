Research

Research

My research lies at the intersection of information theory, statistical decision theory, and reinforcement learning.

I am particularly interested in using information-theoretic methods to characterize fundamental performance limits in interactive learning and decision-making problems.

Information-Theoretic Limits of Learning

A recurring theme in my research is the use of information measures to understand the fundamental difficulty of statistical and sequential decision-making problems.

Quantities such as mutual information and divergence measures provide a way of relating statistical distinguishability to achievable decision-theoretic performance.

\[ I(V;Z) = D_{\mathrm{KL}} \left( P_{VZ} \,\middle\|\, P_V P_Z \right). \]

Reinforcement Learning and Minimax Regret

I study minimax regret in reinforcement-learning problems from an information-theoretic perspective.

One direction of my work investigates how Bayesian regret bounds, minimax duality, information ratios, and mutual information can be used to obtain algorithm-independent minimax regret guarantees.

These methods can be studied in settings including bandit problems, Markov decision processes, and structured reinforcement-learning models.

Interactive Statistical Decision Making

In interactive statistical decision making, observations depend on actions chosen by the learning algorithm.

This interaction introduces statistical dependence that does not appear in classical passive estimation. I am interested in converse techniques that remain applicable under adaptive data collection.

Minimax Quantiles and Tail Risk

Expected risk and expected regret summarize average performance, but they may not describe rare yet consequential failures.

My recent work therefore considers minimax quantiles and high-probability lower bounds for interactive statistical decision problems.

This provides a framework for studying how the fundamental difficulty of a learning problem depends explicitly on a confidence or risk level.

Fano-Type Converse Methods

Fano's inequality is one of the classical tools for deriving information-theoretic lower bounds.

I am interested in extensions of Fano-type arguments that apply to more general loss functions, interactive problems, and tail-sensitive criteria.

Recent work explores generalized formulations based on bounded transformations of the loss and Bernoulli \(f\)-divergence inequalities.

Risk-Sensitive Decision Making

Another direction concerns decision criteria beyond expected loss, including quantiles and Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR).

I am interested in information-theoretic methods for deriving lower bounds on these risk-sensitive quantities and understanding their behavior in interactive learning problems.

Privacy and Fundamental Limits

I am also interested in how privacy constraints alter fundamental statistical and decision-theoretic limits.

In particular, recent work studies minimax quantile lower bounds when the class of admissible decision rules is restricted by mutual-information privacy constraints.
