Raghav Bongole

Doctoral Student in Information Science and Engineering

I work on information-theoretic methods for reinforcement learning and interactive statistical decision making.

My research focuses on fundamental performance limits, minimax regret, minimax quantiles, tail-sensitive risk, and information-theoretic converse techniques.

I am a doctoral student at KTH Royal Institute of Technology and WASP, supervised by Mikael Skoglund and Tobias J. Oechtering.

Research

My research lies at the intersection of information theory, statistical decision theory, and reinforcement learning.

I am particularly interested in using information-theoretic quantities to characterize fundamental limits in interactive learning and decision-making problems.

Information-Theoretic Limits

Converse techniques and information measures for understanding the difficulty of statistical and sequential decision problems.

Reinforcement Learning

Minimax regret, Bayesian regret, duality, and information-theoretic performance guarantees.

Risk-Sensitive Learning

Quantiles, CVaR, high-probability performance, and tail-sensitive objectives in interactive decision making.

Current Research Directions

A recurring theme in my work is to relate decision-theoretic performance to measures of statistical distinguishability and information.

I am currently interested in minimax lower and upper bounds for interactive decision problems, especially under risk-sensitive and privacy-related constraints.
